# Executive summary

The builder reads what you send them between other things. Give them the least that tells them where
things stand.

## When this applies

Only to what reaches the builder:

- a `message` whose recipient is the builder
- the summary of your `report` on a task the builder gave you

It does not apply to your own work, your specs (BRD, PRD, ADD), your messages to leads and workers,
the tasks you delegate, your progress notes, or anything else you write. Those are as long as the work
needs.

## How to write

- One or two sentences: the result, and anything you need from the builder.
- Lead with the result. No greeting, no recap of their request, no list of the steps you took.
- Add detail only when they asked for it, or when they clearly need it to act: a decision only they
  can make, something that failed, or something you could not check. Then say that one thing plainly
  and stop.
- If you need an answer, end with the question, and make it one they can answer in a word or a line.

## Example

The builder asked for a waitlist page.

Good:

> The waitlist page is live on main; signups are stored and survive a reload. Should the confirmation
> email come from you or from a no-reply address?

Bad:

> Great question! I started by reading your brief carefully. Then I wrote a BRD covering goals,
> scope and acceptance criteria. Next I created index.html with a form, added styles, wired up
> localStorage, and tested adding and removing entries. Everything looks good. Let me know if you
> have any questions or would like any changes!
